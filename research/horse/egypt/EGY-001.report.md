## New primary legal and commentary findings

| Author / locator | Explicit argument | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Rambam, Kings3:3 | Chariot requirement allowed; one surplus horse running before king as other kings do forbidden | Courtly display / surplus, rather than universal horse ban |
| Rambam5:7 | Egypt settlement forbidden; three biblical return passages, Alexandria included | Exodus14:13 is among prooftexts; no explicit horse-rule explanation through Pharaoh’s cavalry defeat |
| Rambam5:8 | Trade and temporary return permitted; settlement forbidden | Egyptian commerce not categorically banned; no horse noun in this paragraph |
| Abarbanel, Deut17 | Needed cavalry permitted; commerce/horse purchase not intrinsically banned; reliance leads to seeking Egyptian military aid | Alternative exile explanation retained; political/theological commentary, not archaeological trade proof |
| Kli Yakar17:18, explaining17:16 | Excess horses displace trust in God; other trade allowed; cites Isaiah31:1 | Original anchor is17:18, not a separately printed17:16 gloss |
| Gur Aryeh17:16[12] | Accumulation makes king necessarily dependent on Egypt, unlike nonessential commerce | His claim of selected Egyptian horses is author assertion; independent historical validation pending |
| Rabbeinu Bachya17:16 | Temporary command to avoid Egyptian practices; alternative view limits enduring prohibition to travel from Israel | Substantial disagreement with Rambam’s residence rule; do not flatten into consensus |

Abarbanel’s text offers multiple readings. Main paragraph says excess military reliance leads to Egypt **for aid**, and alternatively to exile. Later paragraph remarks that ולא ישיב need not state the causal reason for the horse ban, contrasting wording with למען לא ישיב. Preserve these alternatives rather than selecting one as the sole historical explanation.

No inspected author in this EGY batch explicitly says that Deut17:16 forbids royal horses **because Pharaoh’s horses drowned at the sea**. Rambam does cite Exodus14:13 for Egyptian residence, and existing Ramban likewise connects that promise with return. This is a limited negative result, not absence across Jewish literature. The sea imagery/prince texts in MID/LAT should remain separate until an author actually makes the proposed causal connection.

## I Kings10:28: מקוה / Qeve issue

Original Hebrew and adjacent10:29 read at [Mechon-Mamre I Kings10](https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt09a10.htm). Hebrew says `מִמִּצְרָיִם; וּמִקְוֵה`, while its paired English translates the second term as **Keveh**, giving a second source. The [KJV10:28 witness](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Kings+10:28&version=KJV) instead renders it **linen yarn**. Both translation witnesses are recorded so the ambiguity is visible. No authority is silently assigned to one solely because it fits an economic model.

The Hebrew reference, geographical interpretation of Qeve/Que/Keveh, variant ancient translations, place-name evidence and possible readings of מצרים must be separately investigated. This batch does not independently establish Qeve=Cilicia, geographical sources, precise breeding centers, trade monopolies, Egypt as sole supplier, anachronistic cavalry systems or prices as archaeologically verified market data. I Kings10:29 itself speaks of chariot and horse prices and transmission to Hittite/Aramean kings; distinguishing literary statement from reconstructed economy remains essential.
