## Results

- commentary:Malbim:2-kings:2:11 — pass_with_clarification. Figure of divestment is explicit, yet Malbim also says light elements carry Elijah's body skyward. Keep both statements; do not turn figurative interpretation into denial of ascent. Retain upper-chariot correspondence. No quote correction needed.
- commentary:Malbim:2-kings:6:17 — pass. Angelic force takes horse/chariot fire form corresponding to Aram’s force; seeing spiritual beings without prophetic preparedness requires a great miracle.
- commentary:Radak:2-kings:2:1 — pass. Whirlwind carries; visible horse/chariot form signifies departure of Israel’s defender. Bodily Eden view explicitly reported as another opinion.
- commentary:Radak:2-kings:2:11 — pass. Radak’s own text says body consumed and spirit returns to upper heavens; Elisha sees a likeness. Do not combine his account with reported Eden view or Ralbag's living-hidden account.
- commentary:Ralbag:2-kings:2:11 — pass. Miraculous fiery appearance assigned a carrying function. 2:1 says aerial height, not bodily ascent into celestial heavens; living hidden destination. 2:10 rejects an interpretation requiring death.

## Bible check

Opened the Hebrew biblical text on both Wikisource verse pages, including pointed and consonantal forms. At 2:11, fire qualifies both chariot and horses; separation comes before ascent in the whirlwind. The verse does not expressly say Elijah mounted or rode the chariot. At 6:17, text says horses and chariot of fire; it does not repeat אש after סוסים. Malbim explicitly applies fiery form to both, so preserve distinction between biblical wording and commentary. The opened-eyes narrative supplies perception context; angel identification is Malbim's explicit commentary.
