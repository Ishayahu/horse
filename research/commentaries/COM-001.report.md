## Findings requiring independent review

- Radak distinguishes the invisible whirlwind that lifts Elijah from the visible likeness of fiery chariot/horses. His own bodily-dissolution account must be separated from his report of a different rabbinic/popular bodily-Eden view. Ralbag 2:11 explicitly says the miracle carried Elijah, while 2:1 says to an unknown earthly/aerial location alive. Malbim reads fiery chariot as a figure for separation from materiality. These are genuinely different traditional interpretations.
- Malbim 6:17 explicitly identifies the fiery equine/chariot appearance as protective angels in a form corresponding to the Aramean military force. It does not assert interworld travel.
- Rashi Zechariah 1:8 identifies the rider as an angel; Malbim identifies the horses as warriors and the red horse as Alexander. Rashi 6:1/5 explains chariots/princes as delegation of kingdom rule. Malbim's four-horses-per-chariot count in 6:5 belongs to his interpretation, not an explicit verse count.
- Rashi Deuteronomy 17:16 connects horses to Egyptian sourcing; Ramban distinguishes trust in God from military reliance and resident Egyptian procurement; Malbim permits needed cavalry horses, forbids idle excess, and reads the return to Egypt as exile resulting from violation. Preserve this divergence.
