# CHR-002 — bounded earliest-witness audit conclusion

The saved chronology table covers 12 named works or edition families and records the actual bibliographic evidence opened for each. This follow-up resolves the finite task by distinguishing three different dates that must not be conflated: estimated work composition, earliest known work-level witness/print, and earliest witness of the selected horse passage.

For none of the selected horse passages did this pass establish an earliest manuscript witness. Positive bounded findings include: late-thirteenth-century emergence for the main Zohar as a work; turn-of-the-fourteenth-century crystallization for Tikkunei Zohar; roughly tenth-century work-level fragments/citation for Perek Shira; and a catalogued 1576/1577 copy of Mabit’s *Beit Elohim*. These do **not** date the exact horse clauses unless the passage itself was collated in that witness.

The unresolved passage-level witness dates are retained as `unknown`, rather than filled from traditional attribution, narrated events, modern website dates, first printing dates, or broad work estimates. Ari/Vital redaction chains, Hida’s *Kikar la-Aden*, Shemot Rabbah 23, and several midrashic strata remain especially uncertain. These limitations affect claims about historical priority or borrowing; they do not erase the verified content of the passages or block a qualified thematic synthesis.

Accordingly, CHR-001/CHR-002 are complete as a bounded provenance audit, while the project must not claim a demonstrated chronological route from Jewish horse imagery to Pegasus or vice versa. Stronger historical influence claims would require manuscript-level and comparative evidence beyond this project’s established coverage.
