## Distinctions worth preserving

- Rashi Song1:9 interprets an assembly of God's horse-camps going out against Pharaoh to save Israel, explicitly referring to Habakkuk3:15 and reporting aggadic books. This is an additional traditional divine military image and should receive independent review. He does not name the aggadic work here; do not promote his report to an independently identified original midrash.
- Malbim Song1:9 separates `משל` from `מליצה`. The latter explicitly makes the divine soul God's mare, bodily inclinations Pharaoh's chariots, and human body the small chariot corresponding to divine governance of the larger cosmos. The horse is a soul-allegory, not a vehicle with a stated otherworldly itinerary. Independent review required before synthesis.
- Or HaChaim Deuteronomy20:1 introduces his reading with `אולי`: horse=evil inclination already prepared for battle; chariot=human composition; large host=evil powers added by sin. Divine assistance permits purification. The concluding paragraph uses kelippah/holiness and Egyptian liberation, but does not assign the horse an interworld transport function. Preserve the tentative traditional proposal and review independently.
- Ibn Ezra Isaiah31:3 glosses `רוח` as angel. Malbim explicitly contrasts fleshly Egyptian horses with God's instruments, angelic spirits. Neither unit states that the Egyptian horses are spirits; neither establishes a literal spirit-horse.
- Malbim Job39:19,25 stresses providential creation for human use and God-given natural war perception. Rashi39:24 explains delight in war. Neither requires supernatural travel.
- Radak Psalms33:17 and Ibn Ezra147:10 explicitly connect God-given horse strength with Job39:19. Metzudat David147:10 criticises a person's reliance on the horse for pursuit. Malbim20:8 makes a stronger, locally contextual claim that the psalmic speaker needs no further war preparations after supernatural salvation; do not export it as a general legal ruling.
- Rashi Deuteronomy20:1 reduces enemy cavalry to one horse in God's eyes. Kli Yakar first reports that interpretation, then advances his own political-cohesion reading: the coalition appears as one horse and is later divided by God.
- Rashi Exodus15:1 says horse and rider remain bound while waters raise/lower them, attributing it to Mekhilta. This duplicates a motif across authorial transmission, not an independent new ancient witness. Kli Yakar reads the elevated rider/greater fall as pride and humiliation. His `א׳ רוכב על ז׳` comment is about divine rule over seven planets, not seven horses; it was not selected as an equine unit.

## Editions, chronology, and limits

Returned edition metadata is recorded per selected source; it spans vocalized digital editions, On Your Way, Metsudah and Wikisource-derived texts. These are online witnesses, not collated manuscripts or facsimiles. In the fetched Or HaChaim20:1 first paragraph, the phrase after `שהרכבת האדם` appears syntactically incomplete; this paragraph is retained in raw evidence but not repaired or used as a verbatim proof. The quoted second paragraph is intelligible and directly maps horse/chariot imagery.

Rashi/Ibn Ezra/Radak are medieval authors; Kli Yakar/Or HaChaim/Metzudot belong to later early-modern commentary traditions, Malbim to the nineteenth century. These broad conventional strata describe the commentators, not the dates of every motif or the modern digital edition. No dates of manuscripts, exact composition years or first-print folios were verified in this pass. Biblical chapter:verse and returned commentary segment are the locators; **no invented folio**. Sefaria title attribution is edition-level, not a critical authorship investigation. Malbim's remark about ancient poets belongs to his own text and supplies no demonstrated borrowing history.
