## Central distinction

**Chagigah 2:1:10 is Elisha ben Abuyah's horse ride, not a celestial horse vision.** The complete long segment was read. Elisha passes Meir's academy riding on Sabbath, counts hoofsteps to recognize the two-thousand-cubit boundary, and retrospectively reports hearing a bat qol while riding on Yom Kippur that coincided with Sabbath. The voice is supernatural within the narrative, but no supernatural quality is given to the horse. Earlier fire in this same long segment concerns Torah exposition at Elisha's circumcision; later fire concerns his grave, rather than his horse. The report establishes textual narrative roles, not historical verification of these episodes.

Adjacent 2:1:7 explicitly gives Yohanan ben Zakkai a **donkey**. He dismounts before Eleazar ben Arakh's merkabah exposition; fire, angels and singing trees follow. That is relevant mystical context, but neither the donkey nor the term merkabah can be silently substituted for a horse. 2:1:8 discusses Ben Zoma and primordial waters; 2:1:9 introduces the Pardes and Elisha; 2:1:11 cautions against investigating hidden things. All four context segments read. Separate Chagigah 2:2:7 uses Sabbath horse riding to illustrate emergency exceptional punishment and calls it shevut.

## Makkot textual caution

Makkot 3:13:2 is unusually relevant: the simile reverses the expected support relation—a mounted hero supports the horse, illustrating God as the world's Place rather than being contained by the world. This is a horse simile in theological discourse, not a literal celestial-horse narrative.

However, Guggenheimer brackets the concluding halakha (the bracket begins in 3:13:2 and closes in 3:13:5). His English footnote65 says it is preserved only in witness **G**, and reports quotation in Emunah weBittahon ch15 (Chavel1964, vol2 p399), with parallels Genesis Rabbah68:10, Yalqut117 and Pesiqta Rabbati Ten Commandments (Ish-Shalom p104b). Note70 additionally discusses parallel attribution. These are **modern editorial reports** stored separately, not independently collated primary parallels. The meaning/date of G remains unresolved here; do not call the passage securely attested in all ordinary Yerushalmi witnesses or independently date it from the modern edition's publication years.
