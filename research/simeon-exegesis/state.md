## Research question and starting point
Luke 2:25–35, particularly 2:29: why would Simeon wish to die just when the messianic events begin? A learned person could welcome centuries of healthy life for new learning, teaching, intellectual and spiritual development. Distinguish canonical Luke from late tradition about a Septuagint translator's longevity; distinguish frailty from healthy longevity. Do not presuppose Christian hostility to life. The original comparison arose in the context of parallel historical analysis of Jewish exegesis of Joseph and his brothers.

## Current results and hypotheses (not settled)
- Early sample emphasizes bodily release, but sample selection and non-independent catena material matter. Medieval Catholic findings Hugo, Albert, Bonaventure, Lyra, Ludolph show yellow/green not a Reformation invention. Blue survives among Protestants (Henry, Gill, Spurgeon); cannot generalize Protestantism, sample is Calvinist-heavy. Green and blue coexist.
- Bonaventure and Nicholas of Lyra are plausible channels of medieval exegesis to Reformers. Luther's favorable attitude to Bonaventure and reception of Lyra warrant investigation, not proof of borrowing Luke 2:29. Research leads: George H. Tavard, From Bonaventure to the Reformers; dissertation Bonaventure and John Calvin: The Restoration of the Image of God as a Mode of Spiritual Consummation; alleged Luther Table Talk WA TR 1,330.1 wording requires verification; proverb Si Lyra non lyrasset, Lutherus non saltasset. Check dates, editions, known reading, intermediaries, parallel argument structures and common sources. Direct attribution is sufficient but not necessary for a plausible influence hypothesis. Availability of Genesis 46:30 at all periods does not explain changing salience; assess why emphasis changes.
- Russian Orthodox nineteenth-century turn toward historical/philological reading could involve Protestant and broader Western scholarship plus inherited Byzantine exegesis. Filaret and Luzin are leads; Barbara Skinner, Russia’s Scriptural “Reformation” in the Late Eighteenth and Early Nineteenth Centuries (2017), https://iopn.library.illinois.edu/journals/vivliofika/en/article/view/550 ; check Luzin preface and citations. Not direct proof of borrowing.
- Dark-green motif not exclusive to post-Vatican II Catholicism: Spurgeon (published 1893, pastoral readiness), Anthony (1982, service to others), John Paul II (1999/2000), Francis (2022). Vatican II as breakpoint is a hypothesis weakened by counterexamples; compare genres and early witnesses; Paul Phil 1:21–26 already provides premise. Duncan 2006 explicitly allows years of continued life but does not assert intrinsic value. Bossuet explicitly considers and rejects the reason to live because Jesus is on earth; Barnes and Maclaren strong contrasts. Distinguish pastoral willingness to live, concrete benefit of service, and positive value of continued self-development; last remains open. Do not infer denial from silence. No verified common use of Phil 1 by all authors; Anthony explicit, others check.
