# COM-004 — Abarbanel and Ibn Ezra on Zechariah horse visions

Normalized **16** meaningful verse-commentary cells from the saved finite matrix (8 Abarbanel, 8 Ibn Ezra); one Ibn Ezra cell at Zechariah 6:4 merely marks narrative continuity and was not promoted. Coverage: 17 present, 3 empty and 13 unavailable-title cells. Missing titles and empty exact verses do not prove absence in print or grouped comments.

Abarbanel reads Zechariah 6 primarily as the four empires under divine government, with horse colours representing Babylon, Persia, Greece and Rome/Islam; he also preserves attributed midrashic/divine-rider readings. Ibn Ezra treats the agents as angelic/mounted-messenger imagery, stresses speed and obedience, and often declines speculative colour symbolism. Neither commentator here describes winged horses or supplies a mechanism by which horses carry humans between worlds. Visionary and angelic agency is not promoted to literal celestial zoology.
