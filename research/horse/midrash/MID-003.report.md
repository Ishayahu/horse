# MID-003 — wings, chariot and divine horse parallel

2026-10-09. Bounded pass completed at transcription level; three primary records, two relations. Historical scans and commentators remain pending.

## Finding

Expanded Shir HaShirim Rabbah1:9:4 explicitly describes divine manifestation upon a horse, qualified כביכול, while Pharaoh mounts male/female and colored horses. This materially broadens the earlier MID-001 context: there is actual theophanic horse imagery in the cited parallel, not merely a speaking Egyptian horse.

The wings clause does not explicitly put wings on the horse. It asks Pharaoh whether he has wind, cherub and wings; it then refers to plural objects flown from between chariot wheels and explains divine riding with Psalm18:11, cherub and wings of wind. The plural suffix has no unambiguous horse antecedent. The coherent contextual reading concerns superiority of divine motion/power to Pharaoh’s exhausted arsenal; pinning the plural to winged horses is unsupported. Divine horse imagery and wind/cherub flight are associated in the same unit but cannot simply be collapsed into a zoological description.

Shemot Rabbah23:14 abbreviates this material and explicitly points to Shir Rabbah1:9 parenthetically. Its short לך clause lacks the expanded naming of Pharaoh and כרוב. This explains the initial ambiguity without proving a historical borrowing direction. Its subsequent talking horse story corresponds to Shir Rabbah1:9:6; that parallel’s horse speech says איפתיסי נעשית לכם בים rather than Shemot’s רומו של עולם אני רואה בים. Preserve this variation; no normalized single quotation.

## Attribution variation

Wikisource MekhiltaEx14:29 gives horse reading to Pappus, Akiva objects and offers joy reading. Shir Rabbah1:9:4 gives joy reading to Pappis and, following Akiva’s objection, horse reading to Akiva. The contrary assignments are real in both read witnesses. Do not present the horse claim as unanimous or standardize its speaker. Mekhilta Sefaria Beeri2019 agrees with its Pappias assignment.

## Additional discovery, not merged source record

MekhiltaEx14:13 explicitly invokes Elisha’s fire horses as analogy for angelic legions shown by Moses at sea. Exact unit read at Wikisource196. Queue MID-004 to extract separate source and compare 2Kings6 context; this strengthens divine-warrior/angelic martial imagery but is distinct from wings clause.
