## Original citation precision

Shir HaShirim Rabbah1:9:4 at https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/שיר_השירים_רבה_א_ט , oldid271137, lines116–121: Pappis first joy-based reading; Akiva objection followed by male divine horse appearance with כביכול, Psalm18:11 citation, red/white/black matching manifestations, martial arsenal, then Pharaoh/wind/cherub/wings challenge. The male divine horse phrase is explicitly present, not inferred solely from כרוב. Pharaoh's female-horse switch is stated; this witness does not repeat a separate God-on-female-horse clause after that switch. A generalized claim that every sex switch receives an explicitly stated matching manifestation would overstate Shir Rabbah alone.

Mekhilta Exodus14:29 at https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/מכילתא_על_שמות_יד , oldid857637, line289: Pappus divine male/female horse interpretation, Akiva objection and joy-based alternative. Thus both horse sexes explicitly matched in this witness; contrary assignment of interpretations from Shir Rabbah confirmed. No borrowing direction or historical priority inferred.

## Wings verdict

Second-person לך explicitly addresses Pharaoh after his arms are exhausted. רוח/כרוב/כנפים express divine superiority challenge. Neither witness says the horse has wings. Plural הטיסן/שמטן supplies no unambiguous horse antecedent; record ambiguity rather than asserting a settled referent. Immediately adjacent riding contrast says divine rider bears what he rides and rides on something immaterial; Psalm18:11 identifies cherub and wings of wind. Associated horse/theophany and flight imagery is real, but winged flying horse claim not established. Likewise no literal divine body or zoological celestial horse species established.

Dating remains unresolved. Traditional commentaries on plural suffix and printed witnesses remain next steps; not required to verify the bounded textual claim. Coordinator may set independent_reviewed for these three MID-003 records after integrating clarification, retaining limitations.
