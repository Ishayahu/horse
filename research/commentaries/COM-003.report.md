# COM-003 — systematic bounded four-author matrix

All 129 direct TAN-001 verses × Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Metzudat David and Metzudat Zion = 516 author/verse cells. 315 returned Hebrew, 100 were empty verse arrays, 101 had unavailable indexed titles. Actual text scanning coverage is 415 present/empty cells; missing cells were title/index availability checks. 27 matching cells reuse COM-002 responses; its remaining 85 cells lie outside this four-author/direct-verse grid and remain COM-002 coverage. 25 meaningful original-language units selected across 24 author/verse cells. Raw discoveries are separate from selected sources; present does not mean deeply read or relevant. No claim of full traditional commentary coverage.

## Access and attribution

112 explicit author/book index requests were made, followed by one default Hebrew book API response for each accessible index. Chapter/verse arrays were matched by numeric coordinates, never compressed. Index title, base titles, dependence, author metadata and response ref are preserved in COM-003.manifest.json; all 516 cells and their exact read URLs appear in COM-003.coverage.json. Complete book responses cached outside the project at /tmp/horse-com003-cache. COM-003.discoveries.jsonl retains only returned matching verse cells (HTML removed, no interpretation). COM-003.sources.jsonl contains 25 selected exact quotes with source parent IDs, segment locations and qualified interpretations. Fetch/build scripts reproduce the batch. Data fetched 2026-10-10.

Per-book default responses do not consistently give a single selected Hebrew version title: available Hebrew version metadata is retained, but not falsely equated to a uniquely established witness. Defaults may combine versions. These are digital edition readings, not manuscript collation. Empty verse cells can reflect grouped commentary nearby. Unavailable title does not prove that an author never commented on a book. No source has independent_reviewed=true.

## Main distinctions

- Rashi Judges5:22 explains hoof loss through star heat boiling mud; Metzudat David describes fleeing the flooding river and forceful galloping. Supernatural battle intervention and natural military explanation should remain distinct. Rashi's statement needs independent review before central synthesis.
- Rashi and Metzudat David II Kings23:11 identify dedicated horses used to meet the morning sun. This is an explicit solar ritual association; neither selected passage describes riding to another world.
- Metzudat David II Kings6:17 calls the servant’s newly visible object spiritual, while the next segment says its purpose is to quiet his cries and protect Elisha’s location; the operative help is the attackers’ blindness. Independent review is needed for this spiritual/protective distinction.
- Metzudat Zion Isaiah31:3 glosses spirit as angel. It does not turn the Egyptian horses, contrasted as flesh, into angels.
- Isaiah63:13 is safe passage through sea depths compared with a horse on level wilderness. Isaiah66:20 is national return to Jerusalem in mounts and vehicles, contextualised by Metzudat David after Gog and Magog. Neither alone states an otherworld journey.
- Rashi Jeremiah12:5 preserves a local political reading and a rabbinic righteous-runner/reward comparison. This is reported traditional analogy, not a literal supernatural mount.
- Other units distinguish army organisation, horse trade, price-derived chariot composition, sexual horse metaphors, battle courage/hair, hamstringing, mule species and fallen battlefield animals. Horse presence is not sufficient evidence for mystical transport.
