## Findings and boundaries

**I Kings10:28 remains textually unresolved.** [NET translators](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=1Ki&chapter=10&verse=28) read Que, while displaying the KJV linen-yarn witness and LXXM transliteration `yekoue` twice. The note reports an alternative identification of מצרים with northern Mutsur, citing HALOT625. The displayed Greek is a database witness without manuscript apparatus: it cannot by itself establish which geographical identification or reconstruction is original. EGY-001 already recorded Hebrew מקוה and the translation split; this pass adds the NET note and visible Greek witness, without adopting a preferred reading. HALOT, Cogan's Kings commentary and ancient-version critical editions remain unread. Merchant language plus prices in adjacent10:29 supports an economic literary reading, but not a verified monopoly, customs franchise, sole Egyptian supply, precisely known breeding centers or reconstructed market prices.

**New Kingdom evidence is centuries earlier.** [Met24.2.11](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544695) is the Ashur-uballit tablet EA15. [Elizabeth Knott's museum discussion](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-amarna-letters) explicitly identifies chariot/two-horse gifts sent *from Assyria into Egypt*. This demonstrates a diplomatic direction contrary to a simplistic always-out-of-Egypt picture. It does not describe Solomon's purchases. Current catalogue dating is1347–1330BCE; the older toah redirect extraction gave1353–1336BC. Both belong to fourteenth century. [Met1985.328.18](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544062), painted limestone harnessed-horse relief, supplies material representation in Egyptian royal processions, not trade provenance. Its catalogue itself qualifies findspot/original location as probable. Museum description of ceremonial replacement of divine-image processions is not evidence that the horses originated in a divine world.

**Megiddo does not independently verify Solomon.** [Norma Franklin's author-uploaded2017 analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330485541_Entering_the_Arena_The_Megiddo_Stables_Reconsidered_Final_Proof), pp87,97–99, reviews the progression from Solomon attribution to ninth-century dating and then eighth-century dating. Her proposed JeroboamII foundation and Assyrian impetus is an archaeological/historical argument, not an inscribed identification of the builder. Her discussion of Egyptian/Kushite horses is based on cited scholarship that this pass has not independently read. Treat her model as evidence concerning later Israel, not retroactive validation of tenth-century monopoly. Chronological controversy has not been exhaustively adjudicated.

**Later Assyrian administration has its own period.** [Shigeo Yamada2005](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/orient/40/0/40_56/_article/-char/en), DOI10.5356/orient.40.56, examines frontier trading/administrative posts, taxes and tribute in ninth–seventh centuries. Article abstract and PDF opening pages were read; later Gaza/Egypt sections were not successfully retrieved. This supports only a bounded comparison with later administrative mechanisms, not assignment of Assyrian offices or customs institutions to Solomon.

## Economic and military-aid readings are distinct

EGY-001's Ramban procurement/residence reading is about buying horses and royal dependence on Egyptian supply; Abarbanel's principal contextual explanation is seeking Egyptian **military aid** through misplaced confidence in arms. Abarbanel also retains exile and a reading that the return clause need not be a causal explanation. Ancient procurement, alliance and diplomatic gifts can coexist historically, but these particular sources do not prove that one traditional interpretation is the original legal cause. No inspected historical source connects the legal ban causally with Pharaoh's horses drowning. No divine origin inference, myth comparison or demonstrated borrowing is made.
