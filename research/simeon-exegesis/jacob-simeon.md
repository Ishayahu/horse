# Matched-author comparison: Genesis 46:30 / Luke 2:29
Purpose: within-author control of era and confession; test whether death readiness, wish, anticipated imminence, and positive further earthly life vary by narrative. Same four colors B/Y/G/D as corpus, but coded separately for each passage. Do not infer a desire to die from an expectation of immediate death; distinguish direct biography from pastoral application. The Biblical text itself states Jacob lived 17 more years (Gen 47:28), whereas Luke does not state Simeon's date of death.

## Incomplete pairs / negative coverage
- Calvin: Genesis 46 commentary moves from v.28 and rejoicing of father/son (natural affection legitimate but should be restrained) to v.31; no standalone explanation of Jacob's death wish. Do not code G/D from that silence. Luke YBG: explicit desire, faith and specific promise; against arbitrary conditional wishes. https://biblehub.com/commentaries/calvin/genesis/46.htm ; https://www.bibleportal.com/commentary/section/john-calvin/20227 .
- Bonaventure Luke II §68 cites Jacob and Tobit (earlier OCR audit); independent commentary on Gen 46:30 not established. MacEvilly Luke (earlier audit): Jacob analogy; no independent Genesis commentary found. Cornelius a Lapide Genesis chapter 46 in online English rendition has no discrete comment on v.30; only v.27→34. https://lapide.org/01_genesis_46.html . Do not infer position.
- Ellicott series has a clear Genesis reading (peaceful waiting) and Luke (manumission) but multiple contributors; establish named authors before treating as a matched author. https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/66/ellicotts-commentary-on-the-whole-bible/genesis/46/30 ; https://www.bibliaplus.org/en/commentaries/66/ellicotts-commentary-on-the-whole-bible/luke/2/29 .

## Findings
Henry, Gill, Benson explicitly value Jacob's additional earthly years but construe Simeon as desiring death. Henry explicitly supplies Christological/eschatological reason, Poole describes greater weight of Christ's encounter. Benson's close verbal agreement with Henry is a confound: not three independent schools; at most three witnesses, two of which may be dependent. Poole a milder contrast (readiness vs wish, no independent D). Trapp and Clarke compare without adequate evidence to assign opposed views. In Benson Luke 2:38 D† pastoral service coexists with Simeon BY: don't conflate genres or characters. This is a small selected Protestant sample, not proof of general Christianity, Jewish versus Christian anthropology or temporal change. Seek ancient/medieval and Catholic/Orthodox matched witnesses; also test Moses Deut 3:24–25, Paul Phil 1:21–26, Anna Luke 2:36–38, Jacob's seventeen years (Gen 47:28). Distinguish value of relationships/service and further personal spiritual development. No new conclusion on latter.

## Checkpoint 8: ancient witnesses and Anna as a control (25.11.2026)
- John Chrysostom, Homilies on Genesis 65 §2, Russian translation read in full around Gen 46:30: Jacob is satisfied by unexpectedly seeing Joseph alive and says enough of living; Chrysostom describes his renewed vitality at Gen 45:27–28, then identifies the patriarchs' life as pilgrimage at Gen 47:9. Code Y (fulfillment); no explicit desire for accelerated death or positive evaluation of the next 17 years. No independently authenticated Luke 2:29 homily in this batch; do not count as matched pair. https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Ioann_Zlatoust/besedy-na-knigu-bytija/65 .
- Origen, Homily on Luke 15 (Jerome's Latin translation of Greek original), Latin text read https://ancientlibrary.net/origen-homiliae-in-lucam/16/ : bodily bonds, but stresses in pace rather than bare wish to be released. Direct Gen 15:15 Abraham parallel and Phil 1:23; BG. Not a claim that Origen denies wish to die. Homily 17 on Anna treats her as example of widowhood and women's salvation; https://openscriptorium.com/read/origen2/031-homily-on-luke-17 (modern translation). No explicit valuation of her further years after meeting Christ.
- Ambrose, Expositio Lucam II.59,62, Latin text https://ancientlibrary.net/ambrose-expositio-evangelii-secundum-lucam/2/ : Simeon wishes release; Anna's character and long widowhood qualify her to proclaim the Redeemer, so every sex and state of life bears witness. Positive function of earthly life, but no statement that she lived for years afterward or should have done so.
- Bede, In Lucae evangelium expositio I on 2:29, 2:36–38, English text pp.66–70 https://www.uecf.net/books/bede-on-luke/chapter5.html : explicitly applies desire to depart to righteous people of BOTH Old and New Testaments, counterexample to a universal OT/NT dichotomy. Anna's devout life and venerable age qualify her as witness; on 2:38 closely reproduces Ambrose II.62 (translator's note 397, CCSL 14:57.810–14). Bede's letter from Acca instructs him to explain Ambrose and supplement from other Fathers https://ancientlibrary.net/bede-in-lucae-evangelium-expositio/ . No duration of Anna's further life asserted. 
- Martti Nissinen, Anna the Prophet in Luke 2:36–38 (2021), in Herald of Good Tidings, pp.216–231, esp. 220–221. Contrasts Simeon's fulfillment and Anna's future-oriented public witness: https://www.academia.edu/77666024/Anna_the_Prophet_in_Luke_2_36_38 ; bibliographic record https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/anna-the-prophet-in-luke-23638/ . Independent modern literary interpretation; does not identify time of Simeon's death or assert Anna's subsequent lifespan. Do not classify as confessional stance on death or add as main matrix author yet. 
- Tolkovaya Bibliya Gen 46:28–30 search excerpt suggests Jacob's increasing detachment; full Azbyka page 403. Luke commentary readable via https://bible-teka.com/lopuhin-bible/42/2/ but authorship of respective sections requires separate verification; not valid matched pair yet.

Anna control: Luke 2:38 narrates public witness at the same hour; neither her later lifespan nor continued witness over years is specified. Simeon himself also blesses and prophesies after his canticle (2:34–35). Distinguish narrative activity from explicit D (desirability of further earthly life), and historical interpretation from pastoral use. Ambrose and Bede affirm Anna's function but do not explicitly argue for additional years. Nissinen's past/future contrast is a directly relevant scholarly interpretation, not new historical evidence. The Henry/Gill/Benson pattern cannot yet be generalized; Bede's statement challenges the broad form. Next investigate authenticated ancient and medieval pairs (e.g. Ambrose De Ioseph / De Iacob et vita beata, Bede Genesis commentary coverage), medieval and Orthodox commentary on both verses, and Paul/Moses controls.
